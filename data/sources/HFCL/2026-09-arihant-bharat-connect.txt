HFCL Ltd
Outlook: HFCL is poised for a structural multi-year growth cycle driven by hyperscaler and data centre demand, with optical fibre
capacity expanding to 43 mn fibre kilometres and cable capacity to 62 mn fibre kilometres by July 2028, alongside 600 tonnes of
preform capacity by October 2028. Revenue growth guidance has been raised to a minimum of 60% for FY27, with ~50%+ growth
considered sustainable, supported by a ~INR 280 bn order book and improving margins (23.25% EBITDA in Q1FY27 versus 16.7% in
FY26). The defence segment offers a second growth pillar, targeting ~INR 40-50 bn by FY30, while data centre interconnect is set
to rise to 18-20% of the OFC segment next year. With firm global demand, sustained pricing, no tariff impact, and ~INR 25 bn
capex underway, HFCL appears well-positioned to capitalise on a long runway of opportunities through FY29 and beyond.
Capacity Expansion Roadmap
• HFCL is undertaking a major capacity buildout across the optical fibre value chain. Optical fibre capacity stands at 28 mn fibre
kilometres, expanding to 34 mn by December 2026 and 43 mn by July 2028. Optical fibre cable capacity is currently 39 mn
fibre kilometres, rising to 43 mn by December 2026 and 62 mn by July 2028. The company is also setting up 600 tonnes of
preform capacity at a capex of ~INR 12.5 bn, operational by October 2028. This will meet ~50% of captive preform
requirements, with the balance continuing to be outsourced from Japanese suppliers Sumitomo and Shin-Etsu.
Demand-Supply Dynamics
• Global annual optical fibre cable demand stands at ~750 mn fibre kilometres against supply of ~600-625 mn fibre kilometres.
The shortage is primarily driven by preform availability constraints. Demand is split across hyperscaler data centres at ~400-
450 mn fibre kilometres, telcos at ~200 mn fibre kilometres, and defence applications (drones) at ~100 mn fibre kilometres.
Demand is expected to reach ~900 mn fibre kilometres by FY29, with the shortage expected to persist. Unlike previous cycles
tied to the 2G, 3G and 4G rollouts, this cycle is structural and driven by hyperscalers and data centres, with a runway of at least
seven to eight years.
Pricing Trends
• Optical fibre prices have risen sharply. D2 fibre for telcos has moved from ~USD 4-5 per fibre kilometre to ~USD 13-14 per fibre
kilometre. A2 fibre for hyperscalers has moved from ~USD 7-8 per fibre kilometre to ~USD 25 per fibre kilometre. Pricing is
expected to sustain given the structural demand shift. Telcos have held back capex for the past one to one-and-a-half years
expecting price softening, but with prices sustaining, large telcos globally have begun inquiries. The next phase of demand is
expected from telcos densifying networks, 6G rollout by FY29, and defence applications.
Geographic Demand Shift
• Current demand is predominantly from US hyperscalers. This is expected to shift to Europe, Southeast Asia, the Middle East,
and Asia including India. India has announced significant data centre capex, with cable requirements expected to begin in one
to one-and-a-half years. Fibre, cable and data centre interconnect constitute ~5% of total data centre capex, split 50-50
between cable and data centre interconnect products.
Defence Segment Growth
• HFCL has developed land-based defence products including electronic fuses, thermal weapon sights, radars, and multimode
hand grenades under a DRDO technology tie-up. The company acquired an aerospace company manufacturing fuel tanks and
pylons for fighter jets, with a confirmed export order book of ~INR 16 bn at acquisition, now ~INR 20.5 bn. Defence revenue
was ~INR 770 mn last year, with aspirations of ~INR 5 bn this year, ~INR 15 bn next year, and ~INR 40-50 bn by FY30. A defence
tender financial bid is likely to open in February 2027. The board has approved ~INR 2.75 bn of capex for ammunition facilities.
Financial Performance and Guidance
• HFCL reported turnover of INR 49.49 bn in FY26, and Q1FY27 revenue was INR 19.15 bn. FY27 growth guidance was revised
from 20% to 40%, and now to a minimum of 60% over FY26. EBITDA margin was 16.7% in FY26, improving to 23.25% in
Q1FY27; this is seen as sustainable, with further quarterly improvement expected. The operating margin for the OFC,
interconnect and telecom networking segment is ~30%, and preform backward integration is expected to improve segment
margins by ~2-3 percentage points to ~32-33%. Blended EBITDA is lower due to EPC losses of ~INR 800-900 mn per quarter,
which are likely to continue for one to two more quarters.
Order Book
• The total order book stands at ~INR 280 bn. Optical fibre cable and data centre interconnect account for ~INR 190 bn, telecom
and networking products ~INR 78 bn, defence electronics ~INR 3 bn, and EPC including O&M ~INR 35 bn. The ~INR 20 bn
aerospace export order book is not included, as the acquisition is underway, with consolidation expected by Q4FY27. Two
large OFC orders are long-term in nature, with prices revisited annually, while the remainder are short-term (2-6 months). Last
quarter alone saw ~INR 40 bn of orders.

Data Centre Interconnect Opportunity
• Data centre interconnect revenue is currently 8-10% of the OFC segment, expected to rise to 18-20% next year. Assembly lines
are expanding from 8 to 14, then to 28 by the end of FY27, and eventually to 56. Margins in interconnect solutions are
relatively better than optical fibre cable due to higher labour intensity and precision requirements. Approximately 85-90% of
revenue is from AI and data centre hyperscalers, predominantly export, with better realisations.
Capex Plans
• Total capex is ~INR 25 bn including ~INR 2.75 bn for defence, to be spent over two to two-and-a-half years: ~INR 10 bn this
year, ~INR 10 bn next year, and ~INR 5 bn the year after. Debt currently stands at ~INR 17 bn (INR 13 bn short-term plus INR 4
bn long-term), and is likely to remain at similar levels in H1 before declining substantially by the end of next financial year as
cash generation improves.
Competitive Positioning and Tariff Impact
• HFCL is competitive globally on scale, bulk buying, power costs, and economies of scale. Hyperscalers do not buy Chinese
products for this segment, limiting competition from China. There is no tariff impact on the company. During the initial 52% US
tariff period, customers were willing to reimburse, and the amount was refunded in Q1FY27 as a balance sheet receivable, not
a P&L entry. Products are being airlifted with cost reimbursement from customers. No order cancellations have been seen, and
customers are firm on commitments given that fibre and cable are only ~5% of their capex.
Revenue Potential
• Peak revenue from the expanded capacity could range between ~INR 110-140 bn depending on market dynamics, pricing, and
product mix. Growth of ~50%+ is considered sustainable going forward given order flow, capacity utilisation, raw material
availability, and customer engagements. The preform facility, operational from October 2028, will aid margin improvement,
with blended EBITDA potentially reaching ~25-26% or better as EPC revenue share declines and interconnect and defence
revenue increases.
