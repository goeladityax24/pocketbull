Shree Refrigerations Ltd
Shree Refrigerations is scaling as a defence-focused HVAC&R player, with management confident of maintaining a ~40% revenue
CAGR over the next 3-5 years. FY26 revenue reached INR 2.53 bn, and the company closed the year with an order book of ~INR 2.7
bn, providing visibility for near-term growth. The existing two plants can support revenue of ~INR 4 bn, implying that another
facility could be required in ~1.5 years if the company continues to grow at a 40% CAGR. Defence marine remains the core growth
driver, with ~INR 1,500-1,600 bn of government-approved shipbuilding orders expected to flow down to vendors, while another
similar pool is awaiting approval. Data centres represent an incremental opportunity, with billing expected to start from FY28. The
company is also expanding into sub-zero cooling applications through its new Aquilon subsidiary, targeting the food & beverage,
pharma and chemical industries. Working capital days improved sharply from 570 to 370 in FY26, and management targets 325-
330 days in FY27 and ~250 days over the next 3-5 years.
Growth & Revenue Guidance
• Management maintained its target of ~40% revenue CAGR for the next 3-5 years, which it has sustained over the past few
years.
• FY26 revenue reached INR 2.53 bn, compared with INR 500 mn in FY23, implying a strong growth trajectory.
• The existing Plant 1 and Plant 2 together can support ~INR 4 bn of revenue at optimal utilisation.
• At the current 40% growth trajectory, management expects the company to outgrow the existing capacity in ~1.5 years,
potentially requiring another plant.
• Any new product opportunity identified over the next 2-3 years could also lead to additional capacity creation.
Order Book & Execution Visibility
• The current order book stands at ~INR 2.7 bn. The company received INR 2.08 bn of orders in FY26 and executed ~INR 1.53 bn
during the year.
• The company expects to maintain an order-book-to-projected-invoicing ratio of around 1.5-2.0x.
• The company has sufficient working capital, capacity and manpower to execute the existing order book.
• H1FY27 is expected to be less muted than H1FY26, with lower lumpiness as a larger portion of orders has already moved
beyond design, QAP and approval stages.
Defence & Marine Opportunity
• Defence marine remains the company's primary growth opportunity.
• Of the ~INR 1,500-1,600 bn of government-approved shipbuilding orders, vendor orders are expected to flow down towards
the end of FY28.
• Another similar pool of orders is currently awaiting government approval, which could begin translating into vendor
opportunities from FY28-FY29.
• Management uses ~1% of ship project value as the relevant opportunity for its HVAC&R products.
• The company currently holds 60%+ market share in its key naval air-conditioning segment, supported by established
registrations, type-tested products and more than a decade of experience with the Indian Navy.
Data Centre Opportunity
• Management is in advanced discussions with a few data-centre operators, but currently has no confirmed data-centre order.
• The company is targeting chip-cooling systems through its technology collaboration with Smardt, Canada.
• Initial revenue contribution is expected from FY28.
• Data-centre revenue is not included in the company's current 40% CAGR guidance, making it an incremental opportunity if
commercial orders materialise.
• The data-centre cooling market can support more than 10-12 major players, rather than being dominated by a single player.
• Management believes that capturing ~10% market share would provide significant additional growth.
Manufacturing & Capacity
• The company now operates two plants with a combined manufacturing area of ~70,000 sq ft.
• The new facility has significantly increased in-house manufacturing capabilities, including laser cutting, CNC bending, welding
and electrical panel manufacturing.
• Backward integration should reduce dependence on external suppliers and improve control over quality, inspection and
execution.

Margins
• Revenue has grown at around 45% CAGR over the past four years, while EBITDA has grown at around 40% CAGR.
• FY26 EBITDA margin moderated from 27% to 21%, primarily due to a change in revenue mix.
• Spares and service revenue declined from 30% of revenue in FY25 to 7% in FY26, which was a key reason for margin
moderation.
• Management expects the spares and service contribution to recover towards 15-20%, which could support margins.
• Higher operating leverage is also expected as FY26's elevated expenses related to new-order design, type testing and
approvals normalise.
Working Capital
• Working-capital days improved significantly from 570 days in FY25 to 370 days in FY26.
• Receivable days declined from ~350 to 250. Management targets a further improvement in working capital days to 325-330 in
FY27 and ~250 over the next 3-5 years.
• The company currently has around INR 330 mn of loans and INR 50 mn of long-term machinery debt.
• Management expects to maintain the current margin profile even if incremental debt is raised.
New Business Opportunities
• The newly incorporated Aquilon subsidiary will target food & beverage, pharma and chemical industries requiring sub-zero
temperature cooling.
• The company plans to develop a dedicated product range for applications such as reactor cooling.
Exports & Aftermarket
• Direct defence exports remain relatively limited because most countries have their own localisation policies.
• However, spares and service revenue can become an export opportunity.
• Management expects to increase the contribution of spares and services from the current 7% towards 15-20%.
